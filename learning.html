📘 Learn About Credit Scores

What is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a number that shows how responsible you are with money. A high score helps banks trust you more!

Good Money Habits

Paying bills on time, saving, and planning your spending are all great habits!

Missed Bills

Forgetting to pay bills can lower your credit score. Always try to pay on time!

Who Checks Your Score?

Banks, lenders, and sometimes phone companies check your score before lending money or giving contracts.

How to Build a Good Score

  • ✅ Pay bills on time
  • ✅ Keep borrowing low
  • ✅ Don’t apply for too much credit at once

What Is a High Score?

In the UK, a perfect score is 999. Anything over 700 is good!

Bad Money Habits

Paying late, spending more than you earn, or ignoring your statements are habits that can hurt your score.

Pay on Time

Set reminders or direct debits so bills are paid before the due date. On-time payments are the #1 scoring factor.

BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later)

BNPL lets you delay payment. Missed payments can lead to fees and harm your record. Only use if you’re sure you can repay.

Spend Within Your Limit

Try to use only a small part of your available credit (often 10–30%). Lower utilisation can be viewed positively.

Hard Checks (Hard Searches)

A ‘hard’ check is when a lender checks your credit for a new application. Too many in a short time can look risky.

Credit Utilisation

How much of your limit you’re using. Example: £200 on a £1000 limit = 20%. Lower is usually better.

Late Payment Impact

Paying 30+ days late can be recorded and harm your chances of future approvals. Communicate early if you’re stuck.

Starter Credit (Age 18+)

Start small: one beginner product (e.g., starter card or mobile in your name), spend a little, repay in full each month.

Electoral Roll (UK)

Being on the electoral roll at your current address helps lenders verify you and can support approval chances.

Avoiding Fraud & Phishing

  • 🚩 Watch for urgent demands, spelling errors, weird links
  • 🔒 Never share passwords or codes
  • 🧠 If unsure, contact the company using official details

How Long Do Missed Payments Stay?

Missed payments can stay on your report for up to 6 years. Good habits can improve your score again over time.

Overdrafts

An overdraft is credit attached to your bank account. It can charge interest/fees—use only when needed and clear quickly.

Smart Card Usage

Using roughly 10–30% of your limit and repaying in full each month is a strong habit for building a positive history.

Too Many Checks

Many hard checks close together can look risky. Space out applications and apply only when needed.

Disputing Errors

If you spot a mistake on your report, raise a dispute with the credit reference agency. Provide evidence so they can fix it.

Minimum Payments & Interest

Paying only the minimum keeps the account open but costs more in interest and takes longer to clear. Aim to pay in full.

Defaults

If you repeatedly miss payments and don’t agree a plan, the lender may mark a default. Defaults can stay on your report for up to 6 years.

Bills in Your Name

Contracts like mobiles or certain utilities in your name can affect your credit—pay on time to build a positive record.

Interest

If you pay your statement balance in full each month, you usually don’t pay interest on purchases. Otherwise, interest adds up.

Check Your Credit Report

Review it at least yearly to spot mistakes or fraud. Dispute anything that isn’t right.
